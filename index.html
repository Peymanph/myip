Technical methodology
What this page is
A fully static, client-side analyzer. There is no backend of ours: every number is fetched directly by your browser from public CORS-enabled resolvers, or measured inside your browser. Nothing is stored, posted or logged by us — check the network tab and you will see only third-party resolver calls.
IP resolution, cross-check and fallback
The primary source is ipwho.is, which returns IP, coordinates, ASN, ISP and timezone in one call. It is corroborated by ipinfo.io/json and by a raw ipv4.icanhazip.com fetch. Public resolvers rate-limit by source IP, so when the primary refuses service the page transparently falls back to ipinfo.io and says which source answered. Disagreement between independent resolvers is itself a finding: it usually means requests are leaving through more than one egress.
Geolocation precision
Public IP geolocation is city-level, typically a radius of roughly 1–15 km depending on how densely the ISP allocated that prefix. The marker is the registered centroid of your network block and the dashed circle is a realistic accuracy band — it is not your street, and any site claiming metre accuracy from an IP alone is guessing. Coordinates are printed to five decimals exactly as the resolver returned them.
Probe 01 — WebRTC
An RTCPeerConnection is created against public STUN servers with a data channel, an offer is generated, and every onicecandidate is collected for ~3 s. Candidates of type srflx/prflx carry your true public address as seen by the STUN server; host candidates carry your LAN address (Chrome replaces these with .local mDNS names, reported as protected). Any reflexive address that differs from your HTTP IP is reported as a leak.
Probe 02 — dual-stack
Parallel fetches to an IPv4-only and an IPv6-only echo endpoint. When both succeed the v6 address is geolocated through the same resolvers and compared with the v4 centroid. Different countries or different ASNs on the two families is a split-tunnel signature.
Probe 03 — locale
Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone and navigator.language are compared with the timezone id reported for your IP, and the UTC offsets are compared numerically. A legitimate privacy tool with a fixed exit also trips this — it is a mismatch signal, not proof of failure. If the resolver did not return a timezone, the probe reports inconclusive instead of claiming agreement.
Exposure score
Starts at 100 and drops only on observed, reproducible findings: a WebRTC address mismatch, a v4/v6 geolocation split, a timezone mismatch, disagreement between resolvers, or an exposed LAN address. Network type (datacenter, hosting, VPN-looking ASN) is shown as context and never scored — running a VPS is not a vulnerability. Probes that could not complete are shown as inconclusive and do not count as clean.
